# A slow Tuesday, fixed, on one page

Every bar has one night when the stools outnumber the customers. That's your game night.

## Pick the night and leave it alone

**Take your slowest weeknight**, with the kitchen open. One game, one start time, for two months. "Trivia, every Tuesday at 7" is something a regular can remember and tell a friend.

**Match the game to the room you already have.** Tables of friends want trivia. Families want bingo, or an early, gentle trivia night. A late crowd that likes a stage wants karaoke.

## Find the person with the microphone

**Most places hire a host.** They bring the questions, the gear, and the nerve. You pay a flat fee for the night. Hosts report $100 to $200 for a typical weekly night.

**Post the night you want filled.** On the Gig Board you say what you want, which night, and your budget, and local hosts who fit can answer. It's free for venues, and your contact details stay private until you say yes. The board is new, so in some towns you'll be the first to post. Your post stays up for sixty days. Hosts nearby who fit hear about it as they join, and you get an email when one does.

## The first six weeks

Expect two or three teams on night one. Some will be your staff's friends. That counts. Hosts report that a new night reaches three to five regular teams in about six weeks, if it runs every week at the same time.

What helps most costs nothing:

1. **Tell every regular, in person.** "We're starting trivia on Tuesdays, bring a team" from the bartender beats any poster.
2. **Turn the music and the other TVs down** when the host starts.
3. **Hold a few good tables** for teams until start time.
4. **Don't cancel in the first two months.** A team that shows up to a dark room doesn't come back.

## How to tell it's working

Count teams, not heads, and count the ones who came back. Five teams where four were here last week is a healthy night. Then compare the night's sales with the same weeknight before you started. Use a month, not a week.

## What it costs you

The host's fee and a few small prizes. Gift cards bring the winners back. Check your state's rules before you make a bar tab the prize.

Finding a host on the Gig Board is free. And the night's money is yours and your host's. We don't take a cut of the host's pay, the prizes, or the bar tab.
