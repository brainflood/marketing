# Your first trivia night, on one page

You don't need a sound system, a stack of trivia books, or a master's degree in anything. You need a room, a screen, and about fifteen minutes of setup.

## Before the night

**Sign up.** The Free plan is a real plan. No credit card.

**Build the game.** Four rounds of ten questions is the classic shape, and it runs about ninety minutes with breaks, give or take. Pick questions from the library by topic, or let it deal you a mixed round and swap out any you don't like. Read every question once out loud before the night. If you trip over it at home, you'll trip over it at the bar.

**Decide how people answer.** Phones, paper, or both. Paper is the gentlest first night there is: print the answer sheets and the answer key, hand out pencils, and nobody has to learn anything.

## At the bar

**Put the game on the TV.** Open the TV page on any screen with a browser, type your five-character code, and it follows the night by itself.

**Get people in.** Phone players scan the QR code on the TV, or type the five-character code in at joinplay.net. Paper teams just need a team name and a pencil.

**Run it.** Read the question twice. Give them a little longer than feels comfortable. Reveal the answer, let the groans happen, move on. Scores go up on the TV between rounds.

## Three things that save a first night

1. **Have a tiebreaker ready.** A closest-number question ends an argument in ten seconds.
2. **Start on time, end on time.** The bar will invite back the host who finishes when they said they would.
3. **Say the rules once, at the start.** No phones for looking things up. The host is always right. Especially when the host is wrong.

## What it costs you

Nothing to start. Free covers three games a month with up to eight teams and thirty players. Your first four nights get more room than that, up to seventy-five players, with no card on file.

And the tip jar is yours. We don't take a cut of tips, entry fees, or prizes. Ever.
